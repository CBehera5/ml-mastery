## Audit rubric and defect handling

Score each criterion 0 missing, 1 weak, 2 clear, 3 demonstrated with a changed-case example; this is editorial prioritization only, not learner grading. Criteria: motivation, beginner terminology, visual explanation, interaction meaning, maths/code consistency, misconception feedback, transfer, prerequisite return, assessment alignment, numerical provenance, accessibility, navigation and truthful scope.

Severity: blocker for leaked targets, mastery bypass, evidence loss or false entitlement; major for incorrect worked mathematics, inaccessible essential control, misleading available status or missing required explanation; moderate for cognitive overload/inconsistent naming; minor for cosmetic polish. Stop affected implementation on a safety/numerical issue and preserve a minimal reproduction. Do not change frozen references or tolerance to make a design pass.

Each issue records ID, route/file, objective IDs, screen/state, evidence, learner consequence, severity, proposed bounded remedy, contract impact, owner, acceptance method and disposition. Suggested owners are curriculum reviewer, ML numerical reviewer, experience designer and accessibility reviewer; no appointments or parallel work are implied. Re-review fixes against the original objective matrix and exact preservation checks.

## High-risk content questions

Can a learner explain the mechanism without recognizing the wording of a finite answer bank? Do small fictional examples look unrealistically perfect? Are probability, threshold action and calibration distinguished? Are subgroup/uncertainty/shift caveats useful rather than boilerplate? Do visual labels match row IDs and units? Can prerequisites be refreshed without restarting a whole track? Does a “Python” surface clearly distinguish trusted displayed code, notebook execution and hosted execution? Does Model Selection preserve validation-only choice and sealed final-test targets?

Use expert walkthroughs and existing regression/reference evidence now. External learner studies, new test forms, dataset replacement, objective/policy changes, and new curriculum require separate authorization. Keep educational efficacy and local editable formative records distinct from accredited or high-stakes assessment claims.
